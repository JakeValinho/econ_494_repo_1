# Research ideas for Class 3 discussion

These are intentionally early-stage ideas to bring to class and refine using the scientific-method framework: observation → question → hypothesis → evidence → conclusion.

## 1. Do liquidity-constrained households spend one-time transfers faster?

**Observation:** Hausman finds a large spending response to the 1936 veterans' bonus.

**Question:** Is the marginal propensity to consume from a lump-sum payment larger for households with lower income or fewer liquid resources?

**Hypothesis:** Lower-income / more liquidity-constrained households spend a larger share of a one-time transfer shortly after receipt.

**Possible evidence:** Use household income measures in the 1935–36 Study of Consumer Purchases and interact predicted veteran status × post-bonus timing with pre-transfer income groups.

## 2. Was the veterans' bonus spent more on durables than nondurables?

**Observation:** A large, anticipated lump-sum payment may be especially useful for purchases that are difficult to finance from ordinary monthly income.

**Question:** Did veteran households disproportionately increase durable-goods expenditure after the bonus arrived?

**Hypothesis:** The treatment effect is larger for autos, furniture/equipment, and other durables than for routine consumption categories.

**Possible evidence:** The replication data already contain spending categories such as autos, housing, furniture/equipment, clothing, food, and recreation.

## 3. Did the local economic effect depend on the local veteran share?

**Observation:** A federal transfer targeted to veterans created geographically uneven fiscal stimulus because some places had more veterans than others.

**Question:** Did cities with larger veteran shares experience larger local spending or employment responses after the bonus payment?

**Hypothesis:** Places with higher veteran exposure had stronger local demand effects after June 1936.

**Possible evidence:** Combine city veteran shares with local building permits, retail activity, or employment measures already used in the paper's replication package.

## 4. Did households anticipate the June 1936 payment?

**Observation:** The bonus payment was known before households actually received the money.

**Question:** Did spending rise before June 1936 because households anticipated the payment, or primarily after the cash arrived?

**Hypothesis:** If households were credit constrained, the largest response should occur after actual payment rather than when the policy became certain.

**Possible evidence:** Estimate month-by-month interactions between predicted veteran probability and interview/spending timing around the payment date.

## 5. How sensitive is the estimated fiscal response to outlier treatment?

**Observation:** Hausman's preferred specification drops households with total expenditure of $5,000 or more, and the paper also reports alternative cutoffs.

**Question:** How much does the estimated spending response depend on the chosen outlier threshold?

**Hypothesis:** The sign and broad magnitude of the treatment effect should survive reasonable alternative thresholds, although precision may change.

**Possible evidence:** Re-estimate the same specification at several expenditure cutoffs and plot the interaction coefficient and confidence interval as a function of the cutoff.

## Most feasible idea

**Idea 5** is the easiest extension because it can be done entirely with the existing replication dataset and directly builds on the code already translated for this assignment. It also gives a clean research design question: whether an empirical conclusion is robust to a researcher-chosen sample restriction.
